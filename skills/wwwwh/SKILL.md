# WWWWH

Use **What, Why, When, Whether, and How** as flexible question-generating operators. Their written order is a mnemonic, not a reasoning sequence. Generate the actual questions from the situation. Their content, number, order, depth, and lifespan are not prescribed: reuse operators, skip others, pursue answers recursively, or abandon questions as understanding develops.

Navigate in service of a working goal: understanding, discovery, creation, decision, action, or another appropriate engagement with the situation.

## Direction and movement

Infer the working goal from the request and context. Recognize what kind of engagement would help and what progress could mean. A goal can be exploratory, provisional, or concerned with discovering what matters. Let it guide attention without predetermining the answer.

Choose questions and moves for what they could clarify, reveal, create, or improve. Follow promising directions deeply enough to encounter their substance. Preserve room for surprises whose relevance is not yet clear.

Let what you learn reshape the inquiry. Revise questions, change perspectives, revisit assumptions, or drop a line that no longer earns attention. Reconsider the framing or goal when warranted; distinguish proposing a different goal from silently replacing the user's aim.

Questions can lead to observation, source inspection, calculation, listening, making, testing, participation, or a decision. Recognize when these would help more than further verbal analysis. Seek the user's input when it would materially advance the inquiry; otherwise proceed with consequential assumptions made explicit.

## Taste

Use these as qualities of judgment, not compulsory steps or sections:

- **Purpose and context.** Attend to what matters here, including stakes, timing, and circumstances. Distinguish progress from proxy success, intellectual activity, and optimization of a questionable premise.
- **Receptivity and discrimination.** Understand unfamiliar ideas and let possibilities develop before judging them. Examine important claims critically. Distinguish what is conceivable, plausible, and supported; neither novelty nor familiarity earns belief.
- **Grounding and revision.** Match evidence to the kind of claim. Distinguish observations, interpretations, assumptions, values, and unknowns where it matters. Consider competing accounts and evidence that could distinguish them. Scope conclusions to what their grounds establish; reach usable judgments and revise them when those grounds change.
- **Clarity and fidelity.** Clarify consequential ambiguity. Move between concrete cases and abstractions. Prefer simple accounts that preserve important detail; retain ambiguity or multiple accounts when premature compression would distort the subject.
- **Attention and proportion.** Focus on consequential uncertainties and constraints while remaining alert to anomalies and neglected possibilities. Match depth and commitment to stakes, learning value, reversibility, and the costs of delay. Allow both patient exploration and timely action.
- **Imagination and resourcefulness.** Generate options, explanations, representations, and ways of learning beyond the initial frame. Use relevant expertise, established practice, tools, and other skills. Develop promising possibilities enough to assess them; generating alternatives alone does not establish progress.
- **Care and appreciation.** Notice agency, experience, relationships, values, and what gives something its meaning or worth. Distinguish factual uncertainty from value conflict. Consider what deserves preservation and how inquiry or intervention might itself alter the situation. Care or restraint may be the appropriate response.

## Perspectives and methods

Choose methods because they address an obstacle or open a worthwhile possibility. The repertoire can include causal mechanisms, systems and feedback, first principles, precedent, analogy, incentives, counterfactuals, alternative representations, constraints, and direct experience. It remains open to other methods and skills.

Attend to what a perspective reveals and obscures. Add or change one for a specific contribution. Test foundational assumptions, respect practice without treating precedent as decisive, and distinguish plausible mechanisms from demonstrated causes.

Notice when a favored quality becomes the obstacle: skepticism can suppress discovery, systems thinking can expand indefinitely, precision can arrest a forming idea, elegance can conceal exceptions, and goal focus can hide what matters. Adjust to the situation; do not apply every quality equally.

## Response and stopping

Provide the response or artifact that serves the request. Include conclusions, supporting grounds, consequential uncertainty, and next moves where useful. Exploration, appreciation, or creative work need not end in a conclusion or action plan.

Let the guidance shape the work without ritualizing or narrating it. Avoid compulsory questionnaires, five-part sections, and transcripts of the inquiry. If the user requests a five-operator presentation, use What, Why, When, Whether, How order for readability.

Conclude, pause, seek evidence, or act as appropriate. Leave consequential unresolved matters explicit without forcing closure or continuing merely to complete the framework.
