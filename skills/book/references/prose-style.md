# Prose Style

Read this before drafting and again before prose revision.

## Adaptive house voice

Write lucid, curious, confident, concrete, narratively aware, intellectually serious popular nonfiction for a very intelligent general reader. Preserve complexity while explaining unfamiliar prerequisites. Be warm without chummy intimacy, sophisticated without performance, economical without thinness, and witty only when wit is earned.

Let the subject set the temperature. Exile may be atmospheric; computing may be energetic and explanatory; conceptual science may be model-driven and exceptionally lucid. Scene, explanation, portrait, chronology, technical walkthrough, cultural interpretation, historiographic dispute, and consequence analysis may coexist, but they must share one authorial intelligence and advance one spine.

## Explanation and movement

Explain what requires explanation, not what merely sounds sophisticated. Use concrete mechanisms, comparisons, examples, causal chains, and carefully introduced terminology. A passage should advance narrative, reveal character, explain, create needed context, alter the reader's mental model, resolve or introduce a productive question, show consequence, or provide earned texture.

Transitions should arise from logic, chronology, consequence, contrast, or an unanswered question. Do not use generic bridge phrases as structural tape. Introduce people and concepts once; let later chapters inherit what the book has taught.

## Openings, endings, and scenes

Vary chapter openings according to material: a documented moment, a puzzle, a mechanism, an object, a consequential decision, a revealing comparison, or direct exposition may all work. Do not repeatedly reset the entire subject or announce what the chapter will explore.

Endings may resolve, pivot, widen, pay off a setup, or leave a productive question open. Do not mechanically summarize each chapter. Preserve cumulative movement and make the final ending fulfill the book's promise rather than merely recapitulate headings.

Use scenes only at the evidence's resolution. Signal inference and uncertainty naturally. Never invent internal thoughts, dialogue, emotion, weather, gestures, room detail, sensory detail, or cinematic sequence.

## Quotations

Quote sparingly and exactly. Integrate a quotation because its language matters, not because a source exists. Attribute where identity improves meaning; otherwise let the note carry verification. Avoid block quotations when synthesis is clearer and more respectful of copyright.

## Anti-style audit

Treat these as warnings of habitual synthetic prose, not a mechanical phrase blacklist:

- “It wasn't merely X. It was Y.”
- “X was more than just…”
- “But the story doesn't end there.”
- repeated “To understand X, we must first…”
- “This would change everything.”
- “In many ways…”
- “It is a testament to…”
- fake profundity or melodramatic emphasis;
- constant rhetorical questions or breathless mini-reveals;
- predictable three-item lists in every section;
- identical chapter openings or endings;
- automatic chapter summaries;
- “little did they know” suspense;
- unnecessary direct address to the reader;
- empty adjectives such as `remarkable`, `pivotal`, `transformative`, and `groundbreaking`;
- calling something fascinating instead of making it fascinating;
- “In this chapter, we will explore…”;
- mechanically symmetrical chapter structures;
- generic transitions, over-signposting, inflated adjectives, vague abstraction, cliché, repetitive sentence patterns, monotonous paragraph lengths, recurring conspicuous phrases, and unnecessary summaries.

Do not cure synthetic regularity by adding random quirks. Revise toward precision, concrete detail, proportion, syntactic and paragraph-level variation, confidence, economy, real explanation, and restrained wit.
