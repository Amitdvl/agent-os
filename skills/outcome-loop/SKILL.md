# Outcome Loop

A loop observes the current state, acts toward an objective, and uses the result to choose its next action.

## Establish the target

Inspect the available context and systems. Define what would count as the requested outcome, what must remain true, and what evidence would distinguish progress from activity. Use a baseline when comparing change; qualitative outcomes can use concrete examples and reasoned judgment. Do not replace the objective with an easier metric.

Resolve routine choices yourself. Ask only for missing information or authority that materially changes the outcome or permissible action; continue independent work meanwhile. Existing authorization applies; the objective does not expand it.

## Close the loop

Use relevant skills and existing tools. Build only what is missing to observe, act, and check results. Delegate when it improves the work, retaining ownership of integration and verification.

Choose the next action for its expected contribution to the outcome or reduction of a consequential uncertainty. When timing matters, compare the cost of delay with the best alternative use of the same time, including the value of waiting for better information. Distinguish evidenced deadlines or decay from speculative urgency; prefer a small action that preserves a valuable option when a larger commitment is premature. Execute it, inspect what actually happened, and use that evidence to continue, revise, undo, or finish. Complete reasonable supporting work; a plan or installed workflow alone is not the requested outcome.

Check results against the real operation, not merely your own account of success. Match verification to the consequences and uncertainty. Consider contradictory evidence, coverage, and delayed effects; a favorable sample or an improvement after a change does not by itself establish reliable impact or causation. Preserve the original success criteria when evaluating attempts.

Bound retries and resource use proportionately. Before repeating an action whose result is uncertain, check whether it already took effect. Continue while a plausible next step is worth its cost; investigate stalls rather than repeating unchanged attempts. Stop on verified success, an exhausted limit, or a blocker after reasonable alternatives. Report the remaining gap without redefining success.

For work spanning runs, retain task state sufficient to resume: objective, evidence, attempted actions, and next step. When ongoing operation or delayed follow-up is requested, use a supported scheduler with a verified trigger, continuation context, and a way to stop it. Scheduling is not evidence of the eventual outcome; distinguish what has run from what remains pending.

## Report to the requested destination

Use Notion only when reporting there was requested or a matching destination
was explicitly established. Without that reporting intent, finish in the current
chat or requested artifact; do not require a Notion project for ordinary work.
A loop does not itself authorize a supervisor, separate chats, or a scheduler.

If `references/projects.local.md` exists beside this skill, read it for an explicitly established project matching the current objective. A matching entry supplies a report destination, not additional authority; a destination supplied in the current request takes precedence. Keep project URLs and private project context local, outside portable skill distributions. If no entry matches, do not invent a destination or block otherwise complete work on a Notion choice. When the user requests a new project, create it in the established Notion project area, verify it, and record its reference locally when requested. Do not route unrelated loops into the same project.

For a requested or matching established Notion report, create or update a detailed report in that destination using the available Notion skill or tool. Resolve an unclear destination while work proceeds. Include the objective, starting state, changes and reasoning, results with evidence links, material failed attempts, limitations, and remaining actions. For an active loop, include its trigger, current state, and how to inspect, resume, or stop it. Keep credentials and unnecessary sensitive data out.

Verify the saved Notion report. If requested Notion publishing is unavailable, preserve the report locally and identify publication as incomplete. For this reporting scope, end with the outcome, any unresolved dependency, and the verified Notion link or local fallback.
