# Sources and adaptations

## Aristotle's explanatory lenses

[Aristotle, Physics, Book II, chapter 3](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/physics.2.ii.html), translated by R. P. Hardie and R. K. Gaye, distinguishes the constituent material, form or essence, primary source of change, and end for the sake of which something is done. Chapter 7 returns to their use in explanation.

This skill adapts those questions to designed artifacts, requirements, plans, and workflows. Treating information and resources as practical constituents, and asking about incentives, beneficiaries, and operational properties, are contemporary adaptations. The four causes do not themselves supply a statistical test, evidence standard, or optimization algorithm. Do not force an intended human purpose onto every natural phenomenon.

## Causal effects and counterfactuals

[Judea Pearl, An Introduction to Causal Inference (2010)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2836213/) distinguishes observational association from intervention and counterfactual questions, with explicit causal assumptions needed for causal conclusions.

The skill borrows the distinction to ask what would change if a component were removed or replaced. Its short causal chains are working hypotheses, not identified causal models; a narrative mechanism alone does not estimate an effect. Randomized trials, natural experiments, observational evidence, expert knowledge, and historical incidents can support different claims with different limitations. A lightweight audit need not apply formal do-calculus.

## Proxy failure

[David Manheim and Scott Garrabrant, Categorizing Variants of Goodhart's Law (2018)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585) distinguishes ways in which optimization of a proxy can diverge from the underlying goal.

The practical adaptation is to ask how the metric could improve while the intended outcome deteriorates, preserve unmeasured value, and include proportionate quality checks. This does not imply that all measurement or optimization fails.

## Original synthesis and limits

The premise categories, counterfactual contribution rule, alternative comparison, sensitivity checks, stopping rule, output brief, and Production Algorithm handoff are this skill's operational synthesis. They are not a validated universal metric or quotations from Aristotle or Pearl.

`J(x) = V(system with x) − V(best feasible alternative without x)` describes a conditional comparison under explicit values, boundaries, time horizon, and constraints. It does not provide universal units or make the counterfactual observable. When aggregation is unjustified, use separate outcome dimensions. Evidence strength is reported separately from estimated value; unknowns are not scored as zero. No checklist guarantees exhaustive variable coverage or eliminates bias.
